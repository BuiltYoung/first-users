# First Users

Made by Built Young (builtyoung.org). This credit is for people reading the skill. Never add it to a founder's messages.

The first users don't come from posts. They come from the founders asking specific people, one at a time, and then helping each one get started. This skill plans that work and writes the messages. Built for Built Young classes and for any first-time founder.

## Workflow

### 1. Intake (ask only for what's missing)

- Product, its link (the tracked one for outreach) and what state it's in
- Exactly who it is for: one group, described so a stranger could find them
- Who the team already knows in that group, and who could introduce them
- Communities where the group gathers, and the team's standing in each
- Founders' age bracket and the adult involved, if under 18
- The goal for this round: 10 users, 10 supporters for a launch, 5 clubs

If the user pastes a `GTM PIPELINE` or `GTM PROFILE` block, take these from it.

### 2. Build the list

Three rings, in a table (first names or organisation names only):

| Ring | Who | Aim for | Why they'd care |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm | People with the problem who know a founder | 20 | They'll answer honestly |
| One hop | People a warm contact can introduce: club leads, teachers, group admins | 20 | One yes reaches many |
| Cold | Public communities and organisations with the problem | 10 | Tests whether strangers care |

The team fills in the names. Never fill a list with names the team didn't give. For founders under 18, add a "Sent by" column: the founder for people they know, the adult for everyone in the cold ring.

### 3. Write the messages

Each message has one specific ask, one link and an easy way to say no. Write them in the founder's voice and the reader's language:

- **Warm:** "You told me [their problem]. We built something for it. Would you try it for a week and tell me what's wrong with it?"
- **Intro request:** who they want to meet, why that person would care, and a two-line blurb the contact can forward
- **Community post:** written to that group's rules, useful without clicking, and sent to the admin for an OK first
- **Cold email to an organisation:** five sentences: who we are, the problem we saw, what we built, the small ask (a 15-minute demo), how to reply
- **In person:** a 30-second version with a QR code

Ask for use and feedback, never for a favour, a like or a vote.

### 4. Follow up once

One short follow-up after three to five days. No reply after that means no for now; leave them be.

### 5. Onboard by hand

For the first five users: sit beside them or get on a call, watch them use it without helping, and ask three questions: what did you expect to happen, where did you get stuck, would you use it again and for what. Write their words down exactly. Ask permission before quoting anyone.

Then ask each one: "Who else has this problem?" That is the next ring.

### 6. Track it

```
Name | Ring | Asked on | Replied | Signed up | Used it | What they said | Next step
```

Give the empty tracker and the numbers to report at the end of the round: asked, replied, signed up, used it twice.

End with **For the run record:** `First users: [asked / replied / signed up / used]`, or `[NEEDS]` until the round has run.

## Founders under 18

- Contact people they already know, or reach organisations through an adult or the program.
- Cold emails to schools, businesses and adults they don't know are sent by, or with, the adult, from a team address the adult can read.
- When the cold ring is another school or its clubs, the adult sends the message, adult to adult: to the teacher advisor or the school office, not from the founder to students there. The founders can be named in it and can join the demo. Students at another school are reached only through that school's advisor or a warm introduction.
- No private one-to-one chats with adult strangers; move it to the team address.
- In-person asks happen at school, at a club or at an event with an adult present.

## Rules

- No mass DMs, no scraped lists, no automation that sends for them. Each message is sent by a person to a person.
- Follow each platform's and each group's rules; ask the admin before posting.
- No paying or trading for sign-ups, reviews or votes.
- Report real counts, including the ones who said no. Ten asked and two using it is a real result.
- Never invent a quote or a user.

## Class mode

For several teams, give each team its three-ring table to fill in and the warm message, and set one target for the week: ten people asked, by name.

## Language

Reply in the user's language. Write each message in the language the recipient uses.
